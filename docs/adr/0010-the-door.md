# 0010. The door: calls on a local socket, the window that answers them, and the answer call

Status: accepted. Date: 2026-11-12.

## Context

A mediator is a value in memory (record 0006). For parties in separate terminals to use it, something must hold it in a running process, take their calls, prove who each caller is, and hand the core exactly what was signed, with the time and the salts the core takes as parameters (`core-no-world`). That is the door, and the process that holds one Accord's mediator behind it is the window.

Three things about it matter beyond its own code. A call must be bound to one caller, one Accord and one moment, so a captured call cannot be sent again later or against another Accord. The decider must see the part it is asked about, which the protocol held back until now: maybe-1 said the held part leaves the mediator only by a release. And a party that receives an answer should be able to show, later, that the decider answered this exact text, which the mediator's receipt alone shows only as a digest.

## Decision

- **The socket.** A unix socket, mode 0600, one call per connection: the client writes one call and a line feed, reads one reply and a line feed, and closes. A call is at most 1 MiB; the door stops reading a longer one and refuses it.
- **The call** is `{"call","sig"}`. `call` holds `op` (`give`, `get`, `questions`, `resolve` or `result`), `caller` (the signer's actor reference), `accord` (the Accord id the caller expects), `at` (the caller's clock, whole seconds), `nonce` (16 random bytes in hex) and `args`, by op. A give's message and envelope travel in `args` as standard base64 of the exact bytes the caller signed. `sig` is the call's signature: `signing.SignPayload` over the call's canonical bytes, under the media type `application/vnd.placket.call+json`, defined once in `adapters/local` (`call-signing`). The core refuses its four type URIs as payload types, so a call's signature can never stand for a placket object's, and no new type URI is needed.
- **The door's checks, in order** (`door-check-order`): size (`too-large`), form (`bad-call`), the signature under `call.caller` (`bad-signature`), the Accord (`wrong-accord`), the time within 60 seconds of the door's clock (`stale`), and the nonce, unseen in the last 120 seconds (`replayed`). The first that fails decides, and the call reaches no mediator function. Only a call that passes all six has its nonce remembered, so a refused call changes nothing, the nonces included. Then the mediator runs, and its first row is membership: a stranger's well-signed call is `not-a-member` whatever its message holds. The door's codes are a type of their own, never a rejection or an error of the core, and the reply carries each under its own member, `door`, `rejection` or `error` (`rejection-vs-error`). The door repeats none of the core's checks (`adapters-no-recheck`), and hands the core the operation's bytes as decoded, never encoded again (`door-passes-bytes`).
- **The door owns the world** (`door-owns-clock`). It reads its clock once per call, at whole seconds, and uses that time for the staleness check, for a tick before the operation and for the operation itself; with no call, it ticks once a second. It mints the 16-byte salt of every accept and change from a source it is given (the binary passes `crypto/rand`), asks for release on every accept and change, and, when a give is asked about and the question has no answerers, finds it undeliverable at once. The clock and the salt source are parameters of the window, so a test can drive two windows with one script.
- **One goroutine owns the mediator** (`one-mediator-goroutine`). Each connection is read on a goroutine of its own and handed to the window's one loop over a channel; the loop runs the checks, the tick, the operation and the journal writes, and hands back the reply. No other goroutine touches the mediator.
- **The replies.** A give or a resolve returns its outcomes, the release's beside the answer's, and for an accept or a change the salt the door minted, so the decider can keep its version. A get returns the delivery as the core returned it. `result` returns a closed question's closing and release to its asker. `questions` returns the open questions the caller may answer: for each its id, asker, kind, deadline and form, and the part, a text as its text and a data body as its canonical JSON; a raw body is shown by its size alone.
- **A core read for questions.** `mediator.Question` (record 0006) gives an open question, its part included, to the mediator's own reference and to the question's answerers, and to nobody else. The door uses it twice: as the mediator, to journal an ask's deadline and roles before the ask's receipt (record 0011), and as the caller, for `questions`. So the door neither matches a term to find who may answer (`one-decision-module`) nor keeps a copy of a held part. A stranger's `questions` is `not-a-member` from the core.
- **maybe-1, corrected.** The requirement now says that the held part leaves the mediator only by a release, and that while the question is open it is shown to the question's answerers and to no one else. An answerer asked to accept, change or decline a part must be able to read it: the first job asks a named person about this exact content, and an answer about content nobody showed them would record nothing. The part still crosses to its receiver only by a release, the asker learns nothing new, and a member who is not an answerer, or a stranger, is shown nothing.
- **The answer call.** A `resolve` call that accepts or changes is the answer call. The door attaches it, as `{"call","sig"}`, the call's canonical bytes in standard base64 and its signature as received, to each delivery whose body is that call's answer: the accepted answer to the asker, and the changed version to the asker and to the receiver. Never to a released proposal, whose body is the proposer's, and never inside a delivery: in the `get` reply and in the journal's held file it travels beside it (`answer-call-attached`). It is never written to the receipts, and it leaves the journal with its delivery. A party that keeps the delivery keeps the decider's own signature over the question, the action and the text, and the receipt's digest covers the same text under the salt the delivery carries. Because parties keep it as evidence, its signed members are fixed: the answer call is registered as a frozen format, by its media type and members, with no schema file. The other door formats stay unfrozen and are written in `adapters/local`'s package comment.
- **What the window prints.** On start the Accord id, the card hash and the operator; then one line per event, the op, the product word, the reason or code and the receipt's id. Never a body, a salt, a seed, a key or a question's part. On SIGINT or SIGTERM it stops and writes nothing; the next start closes what was left open (record 0011).

## Consequences

- An unauthenticated call never reaches the core, and a refused call leaves no trace in the journal.
- The nonces live in memory only, so after a restart a captured call could be sent again within its 60 seconds. The socket is local and its mode limits it to the window's user, so this is accepted for this version.
- The decider sees what it answers about, and nobody else sees a held part. The window itself never prints one.
- After an accept, the applier holds the mediator's receipts and the proposal, and no signature of the decider; after a change, every party that fetched the version can show the decider signed it.
