# 0008. The mediator and reload

Status: accepted. Date: 2026-11-23.

## Context

The capability packages decide: the canonical form, signatures, founding, composition and receipts are each a function of their inputs. Something must hold what those decisions leave behind, between one operation and the next, for one founded Accord: the receipts written, the chain of each pair, each sender's highest accepted sequence number, what is queued for each receiver, and the parts held as questions. It must run the door's check table in order, call composition for the verdict, have receipts build each receipt, and change its state only after every receipt of an operation is built. It is the one role package of the core (`packages-by-adr`), and it must hold no capability's logic (`mediator-no-logic`).

A mediator lives in one process. When the process stops, a door that keeps a journal still holds what the mediator wrote. The next process must carry on from that record so that every chain continues, no sender can give the same envelope twice, nothing queued is lost and nothing fetched is handed out again (`restart-reproducible`, `journal-is-evidence`). Two things cannot come back. A held question's body and salt are never written down, so a restarted mediator could neither put the question to an answerer nor release its part. And a verdict a receipt recorded must not be reached a second time: the decision module may change between the two processes, and a record decided twice could say two things (`one-decision-module`).

## Decision

### What the mediator holds

- **`core/mediator` holds one Accord's state.** A `Mediator` holds the founded Accord, the mediator's key, every receipt written in order, the last link of each ordered pair's chain, each sender's highest accepted `seq`, a queue of deliveries per receiver, the open questions in the order they were opened, and whether the Accord is closed (record 0010). One goroutine owns it; its methods are not safe for concurrent use.
- **Founding.** `Found` takes the founding inputs (record 0006), founds them by every row of founding's table, and refuses with `ErrOtherMediator` a set whose `mediator.actor` is not the key's reference (setup-contract-7): a sentinel, because no set is wrong for it (`sentinels-uncoded`). A founding refusal is `setup`'s `*Error`, returned as it is.
- **The door's rows are one operation of the mediator.** They run in the order of section 4's table and stop at the first that fails; nothing of the message is read before the envelope's signature verified under the caller's key. They are written as one function that reads the mediator's state, not as a package of their own: each row reads a member or the state, and decides nothing composition or receipts decide. A member's give to a closed Accord is `closed`, right after membership. A rejected give returns before any state is touched (crossing-7).
- **A receipt before state.** An operation builds its receipt, and the delivery or the question it leads to, and only then appends the receipt, advances the pair's chain and the sender's `seq`, and queues or holds the part. No test can stop the core between the two, since it does no I/O; the reviewer reads it in the code.
- **Asking** (maybe-1). A give decided `escalate` writes its crossing receipt and holds a question under that receipt's id: the asker, the receiver, the part as its delivery would carry it, the echoed envelope, the subject, the agreed rule, the role labels of the rule, which may answer it, and the deadline, the give's time plus the rule's timeout, at most `9999-12-31T23:59:59Z`. Nothing is queued. Where the agreed rule lists judges, a judge may decide first, and `advise` judges may advise; the mediator takes the judgment as an input (record 0009).
- **Closing a question** (section 6). A question closes once, in the call that writes its closing receipt, and an answer that releases writes its release receipt in that same call, after the closing and before any state changes (`release-in-resolve`). An answer is one signed call (record 0009). An accept's answer and a change's version are each a part the mediator forms; each is decided by its kind through `composition.Decide`, as a part formed by the mediator, like any give (`one-decision-module`). A release after an accept decides the held part again, as given, from the asker to the receiver: a `block` is written as composition returns it, and any other verdict is the answer's pass, written `pass`, `ok` (maybe-10). The mediator compares no stance and adds no verdict of its own.
- **A rejection and an error** (`rejection-vs-error`). A rejection is what the caller did wrong, by a row of a check table: `*Rejection`, with the door's codes, section 6's codes and `closed`. An error is what the caller's program did wrong, or a failure inside: `*Error`. A bad answer is the rejection `answer-call`, for a call that does not read, or `answer-body`, for a text or salt that does not fit it. `Error`'s codes are `at`, a time off the timestamp rule, checked before any row; `internal`, a receipt the mediator could not write; `unfounded`, an operation on a `Mediator` no `Found` or `Reload` made; reload's `journal`, `held` and `question`; and the close's `closed` and `close` (record 0010). None is a code of the protocol. `Index` is the index of the receipt or entry at fault where the code has one, and -1 where it has none; the error's text is the package, the code and the index, never an input. `Err` is set for `journal` alone, to the verifier's `*Error`, and `Unwrap` returns it.
- **What prints as a fixed text.** A `Mediator` holds the mediator's key and every part it queues or holds; a `Resolution`, a `Queued`, a `Question` and a `Judging` each hold a body or a salt. Each prints as one fixed text under every format verb, as a value or a pointer (`secrets-never-print`). The methods take the value, so a copy prints the same.
- **A `Mediator` no one founded.** Every method of a zero-value `Mediator` that has an error result returns `*Error` `unfounded` and panics on nothing. Of the reads, `Answerers` and `Queued` find nothing, and `Accord` and `Card` give the empty string.
- **The same inputs, the same bytes.** Two mediators founded from one set, with one key, at one time, and given the same operations at the same times, write the same receipts byte for byte (receipt-10). Nothing here reads a clock or mints a salt.

### Reload

- **One function.** `Reload` returns a mediator rebuilt from the record a journal keeps, and the receipts it wrote while doing so, or no mediator and the first fault it found. It takes the data first: the founding inputs (record 0006), every receipt in the order written, each open question as the door recorded it when it was asked (`Open`: its id, its deadline and the role labels that may answer it), and each delivery not yet fetched (`Queued`: the member it is for and its canonical bytes, as the mediator queued them); then the founding time, the key and the reload time (`inputs-as-parameters`).
- **What it checks, in order.** The first fault decides.
  1. The reload time, by the timestamp rule: `*Error` `at`.
  2. The founding, at the founding time, as the mediator whose key is given, exactly as `Found` does it: founding's own `*Error`, or `ErrOtherMediator`.
  3. The receipts, by `receipts.Verify` under the key's reference: every row of section 8, the questions and releases of receipt-6 included. A failure is `*Error` `journal`, with the failing receipt's index, wrapping the verifier's own `*Error` (`errors.As` finds it).
  4. Each receipt's Accord id and card hash, against the founded set's: `*Error` `journal`, at that receipt's index. A receipt can verify under the mediator's key and still belong to another Accord the same mediator runs.
  5. Each open entry, in order: its deadline by the timestamp rule, `*Error` `at`; then its question, which must be one the receipts hold open, an ask with no closing, and named by no earlier entry, `*Error` `question`. Both carry the entry's index. Then every question the receipts hold open must have an entry: one without is `*Error` `question` with no index.
  6. Each queued delivery, in order: its bytes in canonical form, an object with exactly the members of a delivery; its receipt id naming a receipt of the list whose verdict is `pass` and whose `to` is the entry's member, with the delivery's `receipt` member that receipt's bytes; its kind the receipt's subject name, and its 32-byte salt and body hashing to the subject's digest (crossing-3); and no earlier entry naming that receipt. A failure is `*Error` `held` with the entry's index.

  A record that ends in its close reloads as a closed mediator and writes nothing; a record cut inside a close is refused with `close` (record 0010).
- **What it rebuilds.** The receipts and their ids, as given. Each pair's chain, from the last receipt of the pair. For each sender, the largest envelope `seq` any receipt echoes for it: only a give the door passed writes a receipt, and every receipt echoes an envelope its sender signed, so an envelope a sender already used is still refused with `envelope-sequence` (crossing-4). The queues, each receiver's deliveries in the order of the receipts that queued them, whatever order they were given in, so that a restarted mediator hands them out as the stopped one would have.
- **What it writes.** Each open question is closed, oldest first, before the mediator is returned: `block`, `timeout`, at the question's deadline when the deadline is not after the reload time (maybe-5), and otherwise `block`, `undeliverable`, at the reload time (maybe-4). Each closing names the mediator as its decider, with that event. Its `from` is the first member, by actor-reference order, whose binding gives it one of the entry's roles, or the mediator's reference when no member does. Each echoes the question's envelope and is addressed to the asker, as every closing is. The asker gives the part again if it still wants it to cross. The mediator's state is assembled from the record first; then every closing receipt is built, in one batch, before the state takes any of them, and a failure to build one is `*Error` `internal`, with no mediator.
- **It decides nothing again.** Reload never calls the decision module. The deadline and the roles come from the open entries, not from the terms, so a closing names what was recorded when the question was asked even when the terms would now say something else. Which receipt is a question, an answer or a release is read with `receipts.Releases` over the verified statements, the one reader of questions (`release-in-resolve`), and the closings it writes are the ones the protocol already has, so a verifier meets nothing it does not already read.
- **Not in the protocol text.** Reload's inputs are an implementation's record, not a format another party reads, and its closings are maybe-4's and maybe-5's. It is proved by the core's Go tests, not by vectors.
- **After a reload**, `Result` reads a closed question's closing and release from the receipts with `receipts.Releases`, as it does for a question closed in the same process, and `Queued` finds each restored delivery by the id of the receipt that queued it.

### Its names

**`mediator`** (section 4, section 6, crossing-1 to crossing-7, maybe-1 to maybe-12)

- `Mediator` and `Found`: one founded Accord and its state, and its founding.
- `ErrOtherMediator`: a set whose mediator is not the key's.
- `Mediator.Give`: a give, by the door's rows, then composition with the judgment given, then the receipt, then the state.
- `Mediator.Get`: the first delivery queued for the caller, removed from its queue, so the mediator keeps no body or salt of it once fetched.
- `Mediator.Resolve` and `Resolution`: an answer, by section 6's rows, from the signed call, the answer's text and the answerer's salt.
- `Mediator.Undeliverable`: the finding that nobody can be reached to answer a question.
- `Mediator.Tick`: the passing of time; every question whose deadline is due closes `timeout`, in the order opened. It refuses nothing.
- `Mediator.Answerers`: an open question's answerers, in actor-reference order, empty and not nil when nobody holds a role it names.
- `Mediator.Queued` and `Queued`: a copy of the delivery a receipt queued, while it is unfetched, and the member it is for.
- `Mediator.Result`: a closed question's closing and its release, to its asker only.
- `Mediator.Question` and `Question`: a copy of an open question, its part included, to the mediator's own reference and to its answerers only.
- `Mediator.Receipts` and `Mediator.Pending`: copies of every receipt written, and the ids of the open questions.
- `Mediator.Accord` and `Mediator.Card`: the Accord id and the card hash.
- `Outcome`: a receipt the mediator wrote, with its verdict, reason and id.
- `Reload` and `Open`: a mediator rebuilt from its record, and an open question as the door recorded it.
- `Rejection`, `Rejection.Error` and `RejectionCode`, with the door's codes `NotAMember`, `EnvelopeSignature`, `NotCanonical`, `EnvelopeHash`, `EnvelopeSequence`, `ExtensionMissing`, `ReceiverUnknown`, `AccordMismatch`, `OnePart`, `OneBody`, `URLUnsupported` and `PartMetadata`, and section 6's `AnswerCall`, `NoOpenQuestion`, `NotAnAnswerer` and `AnswerBody`: what the caller did wrong.
- `Error`, `Error.Error`, `Error.Unwrap` and `ErrorCode`, with `CodeAt`, `CodeInternal`, `CodeUnfounded`, `CodeJournal`, `CodeHeld` and `CodeQuestion`: what the caller's program did wrong, a record reload refuses, or a failure inside.
- `Mediator.Format`, `Mediator.GoString`, `Mediator.String`, `Resolution.Format`, `Resolution.GoString`, `Resolution.String`, `Queued.Format`, `Queued.GoString`, `Queued.String`, `Question.Format`, `Question.GoString` and `Question.String`: each prints as a fixed text.
- `Extension`, `RoleUser` and `RoleAgent`: the crossing extension's URI, and the roles of a crossing message (record 0005).
- `SaltBytes`: the length of every salt of a part, 32 bytes (record 0005).

`Question` is read by two callers only. The mediator's own reference reads any open question before membership is asked: the door that serves the mediator journals each ask's deadline and roles from it before the ask's receipt, so that a reload takes them from the journal and decides nothing again, and a question nobody can answer is journaled like any other. Every other caller goes through section 6's rows in order: a stranger is `not-a-member`; a closed or unknown id is `false` with no error, as `Resolve` tells a stranger to the role nothing of a closed question; and a member who is not one of its answerers is `not-an-answerer`. So an answerer can be shown the part it is asked about, which maybe-1 allows, while no door matches terms or holds a body of its own (`one-decision-module`). `Question` never prints its part.

## Consequences

- A vector can test a give end to end through one mediator, and a door adds only what touches the world: the socket, the clock, the journal.
- The door's rows live beside the state they read. A reviewer checks that none of them compares a stance, matches a term or builds a receipt.
- A door that journals the founding set, each receipt, each ask's deadline and roles, and each delivery until it is fetched can stop at any moment between two calls and carry on with the same chains and the same sequence numbers.
- A question open at a stop is lost to its asker, by design, and the record says so with a closing receipt.
- A record edited by hand, cut in the middle, or mixed with another Accord's is refused with the index of the first receipt or entry at fault; a record cut after its last receipt reloads, as section 8 allows, and only reconciliation against the parties' own records shows what was cut.
- Two reloads of one record at one time return byte-identical mediators.
